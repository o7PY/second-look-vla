Part 1 · The detector

Can a camera tell what changed?

After a robot fails, the first thing worth knowing is whether the failure moved anything. DeltaDINO is the tool we built to answer that from two photos: one from before the trouble and one from now.

The headline numbers, for model C (the version the closed loop uses): the mean of 3 training runs on the test set, each run with its own decision threshold

0.999AUROC on the test set. 1.0 is perfect, 0.5 is a coin flip.
99.3%of big changes caught (over 2 cm or 15°)
0.08%false alarms on look-alikes: scenes where only the arm moved, or the arm hid an object

What it does

DeltaDINO is a change detector. It sees two photos at once and, for every small patch of the picture, asks whether something other than the robot arm moved there. It answers with a 16×16 map of where it thinks things changed, plus one overall yes or no.

1 · Two photosBefore the trouble, and now.
→
2 · Shared encoderOne small pretrained vision model (DINOv2-small, tuned with LoRA) reads both.
→
3 · CompareFor each patch: before, now, and how much they differ.
→
4 · DecideA small head scores every patch, then the whole image.

The version used in the closed loop (“model C”) hides only 81 background patches where a changed object never appears in training. It does not hide the robot arm. It has about 0.89 M trainable parameters and runs in under 5 ms per pair (one pair at a time, bf16, on a laptop RTX 4070 GPU).

Why this design. We believe the pairing of a Siamese two-photo comparison with a gate that decides between retrying and calling a human is new, though we have not done a full literature check (related work was read at abstract level only).

How well does it work?

It is very good where it was trained, and a little weaker on tasks it has never seen. On 200 fresh SmolVLA episodes in simulation that were kept sealed until the end and scored once, model C reached an AUROC of 0.993 (mean of 3 training runs).
