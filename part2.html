Second Look

Part 2 · The closed loop

Does a second look make the robot succeed more?

We put the whole loop together (alarm, pause, look, then retry or flag) and ran 900 episodes in simulation to find out, 720 of them on the eight tasks the system was tuned for. The questions and the analysis were written down before the test ran.

The headline numbers, on the eight seen tasks

−2.9 ppchange in success compared with doing nothing (65.0% against 67.9%)
15.4%of runs fail silently, down from 32.1%
80.1%of flagged runs were ones the robot would have failed anyway

Silent failure means the robot fails and nobody is told. Lowering it by flagging everything would be easy, so we always read it next to how precise the flags are.

The alarm comes early enough

Before any recovery, the alarm itself has to fire in time. On calibration runs it caught 86.1% of the failing episodes before time ran out. The price is false alarms: on runs that went on to succeed anyway, the alarm still fired in 9.1% (the threshold aimed for 10%, and was set on these same runs). The first alarm came at about step 105, with a median of 195 of the 300 steps still left to act.

The three pre-registered tests

H1 Does Second Look beat doing nothing on success?−2.9 ppnot supported
H2 Fewer silent failures than blind retry, without losing success?silent failures −14.2 pp, but success −3.8 ppnot supported
H3 Does it beat alarms raised at random moments?+11.0 ppsupported

The random-timing arm is itself harmful, so H3 shows the alarm times the check better than chance. It does not show that Second Look beats doing nothing.

Why it did not help

Two findings explain it. First, the camera is not the problem: when the simulator’s own truth replaced the camera (the oracle arm), the result was the same (65.0% against 65.0%). Second, “something changed” often just means the robot made progress, such as moving the very object it was asked to move. Changed is not doomed. So Second Look rescued 16 runs but stopped 37 that would have finished.

What we learned

  • Second Look makes failures visible: silent failures fall from 32.1% to 15.4%, and most flags are on runs that really fail.
  • It does not raise success. The effect is a small loss, and we report it as it came out.
